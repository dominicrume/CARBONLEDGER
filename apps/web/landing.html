The problem

Carbon markets are broken by design, not by a few bad actors.

Many sets of books

There are about 26 carbon registries, each keeping its own books. The same tonne can turn up in two of them, and nobody can see it.

Nobody keeps the total

The market counts small reductions, one project at a time. Nobody keeps the running total of what each country has put into the atmosphere.

Trust me, not check me

Buyers are asked to trust a certificate. They cannot check for themselves which tonne they bought, or whether it was sold before.

How it works

The rules live in the code. Every gram is retired once.

  1. Measure

    The app estimates the CO₂ of a basket, a journey or a day, in grams, rounded up. Every line shows where its figure comes from.

  2. Retire

    The server prices the items itself and retires that many pilot units. Each gram has its own serial number and can be retired only once.

  3. Record

    You get a receipt, sealed to the one before it. Receipts are gathered into one fingerprint that is written to Solana devnet.

  4. Back it next

    A named verifier attests a real removal project, with field video and satellite evidence for each tonne. Until then, units are pilot units.

Each country's share is sized from cumulative CO₂ since 1850 (Global Carbon Project, via Our World in Data), scaled to the IPCC's 2,400 billion tonne total.

The app

Retire pilot units for everyday life, one gram at a time.

Scan your shopping

Scan a barcode or pick from the shelf. Figures come from international sources first, then national ones, then recognised organisations, and a cautious high guess only as a last resort.

Log a journey

Flights between airports, or a car, train or bus trip by distance. Flights include the warming effect of high-altitude emissions.

Add up a day

Home electricity and gas from your meter readings, and your meals, for a day, a week or a month.

Check it yourself

Don't trust us. Check the record.

  • Every gram has a serial number, and a gram is retired once.
  • Receipts are hash-chained: an edit, a deletion or a swap breaks the chain at that point.
  • Refused attempts are kept on the record with equal weight.
  • One fingerprint per batch of receipts is anchored on Solana devnet. Only hashes go on-chain, never names, places or baskets.
  • Any receipt can be verified in the app or the browser, without an account.
  • The registry rules pass the same 10 test cases in the server, the database and the on-chain program.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is this a crypto investment?

No. A retired unit cannot be sold or transferred. It is a record that a gram was retired, once.

Do I need a crypto wallet?

No. The app records everything for you. You never hold a key or pay a network fee.

What does it cost?

The demo price is £100 a tonne, from a single gram. No payment is taken: this is a pilot on a test network.

Are these real carbon credits?

Not yet. They are pilot units, not yet verified credits. The record is real; the backing comes when a named verifier attests a real project.

What happens to a refused retirement?

It is kept in the receipt chain with a refusal receipt, so an auditor can see what was attempted and stopped. No units are retired.

Where do the country figures come from?

Cumulative CO₂ since 1850 from the Global Carbon Project, via Our World in Data, scaled to the IPCC's 2,400 billion tonne total.
