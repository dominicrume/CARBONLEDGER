The problem

Today's carbon market is broken by design.

The voluntary carbon market moves billions a year, yet buyers still can't be sure that a tonne they paid for was removed, stays removed, and wasn't sold to someone else too.

Credits don't last

Credits are issued year by year, but CO₂ stays in the air for centuries. A forest that burns down in thirty years has stored nothing, even though its credits were already used.

No one keeps the total

The market counts small reductions. Nobody counts the full amount each country has ever emitted, so nobody can say how much is left to clear.

Counting twice is easy

When credits can be traded, re-sold and checked only once in a while, the same tonne gets claimed twice and greenwashing pays. More rules haven't fixed it.

How it works

One token per tonne. Divisible to the gram. Never traded.

CarbonLedger starts from the total. Each country's historic emissions, from the Global Carbon Project via Our World in Data, become a pool of carbon debt. People and companies clear that debt one gram at a time.

The world's carbon debt

Clear whole countries, smallest first.

Every country has its own pool, sized from its cumulative CO₂ since 1850 (Global Carbon Project, via Our World in Data), scaled to the IPCC's 2,400 billion tonne total. Today each pool is a pilot target.

The plan is to make clearing that debt a shared goal. Members work through the smallest pools first and earn badges and levels others can see, or retire for any country they choose. People and companies can also set their own targets.

Why you can trust it

The rules are written into the code, not a policy document.

The registry program enforces these rules. It is deployed on Solana devnet and passes all ten registry test cases. The app does not call it yet: today it anchors receipts by memo.

attested before issued

Nothing issued without a verifier

A named verifier must attest a project before any units are issued from it, and issued can never exceed attested.

serial ranges

Retired once, for good

Every retirement takes the next block of serial numbers. A gram that has been retired can never be retired again.

one external link

No credit counted twice

A credit from another registry can be linked to the ledger once only, so the same tonne can't be counted in two places.

contiguous anchors

No gaps in the record

Each anchored batch of receipts starts exactly where the last one ended, so nothing can be slipped in or left out.

suspend lever

A failing project can be stopped

If a project's evidence fails, it can be suspended, and no more units are issued or retired from it.

public by default

Anyone can check

Every action is readable on standard Solana explorers. Refused retirements stay on the record too.

Permanence · the plan

Built to last as long as the carbon does.

A permanent record only means something if the carbon removal behind it is permanent too. CarbonLedger plans to back the ledger with a physical removal method and a legal structure that holds the assets for good.

Custodian

A permanent foundation

A Liechtenstein foundation (Stiftung) would acquire and hold the land and physical removal assets. By law it couldn't be dissolved, sold or turned to another purpose.

  • It would hold a golden share, so the mission couldn't be changed by any later owner or investor.
  • Part of the income from every CLT would go to buying land that the foundation keeps for good.
  • The guarantee would come from the law, not from a contract that could be renegotiated.
Operations

Operating companies

An international operating company would run the ledger, the app and day-to-day partnerships, with a UK company serving UK customers. They would run the service but never own the permanent assets.

Record

The public ledger

Every receipt's fingerprint is written to Solana, today on devnet, a test network. The record stays readable even if the ledger later moves to its own network.

Removal method

Biochar

Plant-based material is heated without oxygen, which turns its carbon into a stable solid that lasts for centuries.

Feedstock

Paper that can't be recycled

The biochar would be made from end-of-line paper that would otherwise go to landfill or be burned. Taking it in would also earn a waste-handling fee.

Evidence

Traceable to the project

Each project will have its own ID. Its field video, satellite imagery, batch serial numbers and other evidence will be published under that ID, so every tonne can be followed back.

Working today

Each retirement in the app produces a receipt that is chained to the one before it. The receipt's fingerprint is written to Solana devnet as a memo transaction. Anyone can rerun the chain check at the public audit endpoint.

Next step

The registry program, with verifier attestation, serial-range retirement and external-credit links, is deployed and tested. The next build connects the app to it, so retirements are recorded by the program instead of anchored by memo.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is CLT a cryptocurrency investment?

No. Retired units can't be sold, traded or transferred, and they have no market price. A retirement is a record that grams of carbon debt were retired, once. Today they are pilot units, not yet verified credits.

Do I need a crypto wallet or any crypto?

No. The system pays every network fee and handles the blockchain side. You just use the app.

How much does it cost?

The demo price is £100 per tonne of CO₂, which works out at one penny per 100 grams. You can retire as little as one gram. No payment is taken in the pilot.

Is this real money today?

Not yet. The live demo runs on Solana devnet, a free public test network, and payments are simulated. The receipts and on-chain anchors are real and can be checked.

Why is a refused retirement on the record?

Because a public ledger has to show everything. When a retirement is refused, the refusal and its reason are kept in the receipt chain instead of quietly disappearing.

Where do the country figures come from?

From the Global Carbon Project, via Our World in Data: each country's cumulative CO₂ since 1850, scaled to the IPCC's 2,400 billion tonne total. The source is credited in the app.

Can I check a receipt myself?

Yes. Paste a receipt id into Verify in the app, or download every receipt and rerun the chain check yourself. No account is needed.
